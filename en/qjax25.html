25.1 Assignment error is an affine map in m-qg

With \(e_{01}=P(\text{read }1|0)\), \(e_{10}=P(\text{read }0|1)\), noisy readout is an affine map on the m-qg axis: \[m'=(1-e_{01}-e_{10})\,m+1000\,(e_{10}-e_{01}),\qquad m^\star=\frac{1000\,(e_{10}-e_{01})}{e_{01}+e_{10}}\ \ (\text{fixed point, zero bias}).\] Instance \(e_{01}=0.02,e_{10}=0.06\): \(-1000\to-880\), \(0\to+40\), \(+1000\to+960\) m-qg; \(m^\star=+500\) m-qg. The pull effect is the contraction \(0.92\) plus the shift \(+40\) m-qg; the cost of an \(X\) gate measured in §168 (\(1.64\) linear m-qg) is this same map with \(e\sim10^{-3}\) (exact, tested on hardware §168).

Integrated demonstration (readout confusion). The scalar form is the m-qg projection of the classical assignment matrix \(p'=Rp\) for one qubit, with \(R_{1|0}=e_{01}\), \(R_{0|1}=e_{10}\). Matrix/pseudoinverse inversion is the standard starting point for readout mitigation, but it amplifies statistical fluctuations when \(R\) is ill-conditioned or has large off-diagonal components; multiqubit corrections therefore use constraints/regularization (Nachman et al., npj Quantum Inf. 6, 84, 2020, DOI 10.1038/s41534-020-00309-7; Bravyi et al., Phys. Rev. A 103, 042605, 2021, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.103.042605).

25.2 Echo inversion: quantitative criterion of §108

Echo calibration (circuits \(I\) and \(X\), or \(XX\) echo) estimates \((e_{01},e_{10})\) with \(N_{\text{cal}}\) shots and allows the map to be inverted. Its cost enters \(N_{\mathrm{cal}}\) and \(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{cal}}\); the calibrated inverse should not be extrapolated outside the regime where the matrix is stable and temporal drift is negligible. Inversion removes bias in the model, but amplifies variance by \((1-e_{01}-e_{10})^{-2}\) and adds calibration variance. Decision: \[\text{invert}\iff \underbrace{b^2+\sigma_m^2}_{\text{MSE}_{\text{raw}}}>\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_m^2}{(1-e_{01}-e_{10})^2}+\mathrm{Var}_{\text{cal}}}_{\text{MSE}_{\text{inv}}},\qquad b=m'-m,\ \ \sigma_m=1000\sqrt{(1-qg_Z'^2)/N}.\]

Integrated demonstration (overhead and scope). This local trade-off quantifies §108: it is not a general prohibition on readout correction, but an MSE rule. The overhead is consistent with the \(\Gamma^2\) factor of unbiased measurement-mitigation estimators (Bravyi et al., 2021) and with overhead/sampling in scalable readout-mitigation methods (Nation et al., PRX Quantum 2, 040326, 2021, DOI 10.1103/PRXQuantum.2.040326). In multiqubit distributions, naive inversion can produce negative quasiprobabilities or oscillations; IBU/least-squares/non-negativity are common responses (Nachman 2020). The criterion here covers the scalar/one-qubit or m-qg-axis case where the MSE comparison is closed and auditable.

25.3 Drag-free readout (F7): the sector filter lowers the error from O(e) to O(e01e10)

In the weight-1 sector, one readout flip takes the string out of the sector (the filter removes it); reaching another weight-1 string requires two flips, probability \(e_{10}e_{01}\). Maximum filtered bias: \(0.0013\) (\(\approx e_{01}e_{10}=0.0012\)) versus the first-order \(0.060\) without filter (\(\times46\)); with \(e=0.01\): \(\times196\). Combined with Ch. 24: the filter removes \(T_1\) exactly and the readout to second order without calibrating or inverting — the “drag-free readout” (exact for the independent-flip model; hardware unverified).

Integrated demonstration (order scope). The \(O(e_{01}e_{10})\) result assumes independent readout flips and postselection to the weight-1 sector. It is a property of the code/sector, not of physical hardware: readout correlations, crosstalk or leakage break the independence hypothesis and must be measured before declaring the order. The reported \(\times46\) gain is exact under that model; on hardware it remains unverified.
