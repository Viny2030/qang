Chapter 44 — Quantum Agentic AI Read with qang: Born Policies, the Angle as Natural Gradient, the Decision Reading and Shot Allocation Across Tools

Día de análisis y actualización: 23 de noviembre de 2026

Analysis and update date: November 23, 2026

Abstract. What does qang give a hybrid autonomous agent, and what does it not? The minimal agent: two actions whose policy is the measurement of one qubit, \(\pi(0)=\cos^2(\theta/2)\). Results, all with a verifier: (i) the policy’s Fisher information is 1 for every \(\theta\), and the gradient flow in \(\theta\) is exactly the natural-gradient flow of the logit, \(\phi_t=\phi_0+\eta\Delta t\) with \(\Delta=r_0-r_1\): the worse action falls to \(1\%\) in \(4.6/(\eta\Delta)\) steps, against \(108/(\eta\Delta)\) for plain gradient in the logit (\(23.5\times\)); (ii) near a pole the gradient vanishes and its variance does not, so the gradient sign costs 100 times more episodes at \(\theta=0.05\) than at the equator, and an agent at the pole never moves without an exploration floor; (iii) the decision reading (\(qg_Z\), \(qg_S\), \(qg_{SNR}\), shots for the sign) is computed before acting; (iv) splitting the shots among \(K\) readings with \(n_i\propto\sin\theta_i\) lowers the sum of variances by the factor \(K\sum\sin^2\theta_i/(\sum\sin\theta_i)^2\). Status: exact; no measured gain is claimed, and a single-qubit policy is indistinguishable from a classical biased coin. Module: qang.qagent (NumPy). Verification: tools/verify_cap44.py.

44.1 Reading rules

  1. Every result comes with its function in qang.qagent and its line in tools/verify_cap44.py. Scales: \(m\text{-qg}=1000\,qg_Z\) (reading); \(\theta\) in radians, with \(\pi/2\) rad \(=250\) qga and 1 turn \(=1000\) qga.
  2. We optimize in \(\theta\), never in \(qg_Z\) without regularization: the flat gradient in \(qg_Z\) diverges at the poles (Ch. 34, THEORY_CORE.md §2.1). That negative result is respected here and explained with a formula (§44.2).
  3. Rewards, steps and the exploration floor are parameters of the model, not measurements. Where a number appears, it comes from the formula with that parameter.
  4. No measured gain status: the chapter does not compare against a classical agent trained in a real environment, and does not repeat hypotheses already refuted in the book (Ch. 3, Ch. 34). The audited inventory of domains, with dataset and without-qang baseline, is in book/en/appendix_h.qmd (Open and rejected proposals) and tools/check_baselines.py.

44.2 Theoretical foundations and mathematical formulation

The minimal agent. A two-action agent prepares \(R_Y(\theta)|0\rangle\) and measures in \(Z\); the action is the outcome. Then

\[ \pi(0|\theta)=\cos^2\frac\theta2=p_0,\qquad \pi(1|\theta)=\sin^2\frac\theta2,\qquad qg_Z=\cos\theta=\pi(0)-\pi(1), \]

and the decision state is the point on the Bloch sphere; the policy is a meridian. With mean rewards \(r_0,r_1\) and \(\Delta=r_0-r_1\), the expected return is \(J(\theta)=r_0\pi(0)+r_1\pi(1)\) and

\[ \partial_\theta J=-\frac{\Delta\sin\theta}{2},\qquad \partial_\theta\log\pi(0)=-\tan\frac\theta2,\qquad \partial_\theta\log\pi(1)=\cot\frac\theta2 . \]

Fisher information. \(I(\theta)=\pi(0)\tan^2\tfrac\theta2+\pi(1)\cot^2\tfrac\theta2=\sin^2\tfrac\theta2+\cos^2\tfrac\theta2=1\) for every \(\theta\): it is the Fubini–Study metric on the meridian (Ch. 9). In \(qg_Z\) the same information is \(1/(1-qg_Z^2)\), which diverges at the poles; that is why the flat gradient in \(qg_Z\) is useless and the natural one in \(qg_Z\) coincides with the Euclidean one in \(\theta\) (Ch. 34).

The gradient flow. With the exact gradient, \(\dot\theta=\eta\,\partial_\theta J=-\tfrac{\eta\Delta}{2}\sin\theta\), which integrates in closed form:

\[ \tan\frac{\theta_t}{2}=\tan\frac{\theta_0}{2}\,e^{-\eta\Delta t/2}. \]

With the logit \(\phi=\ln\frac{\pi(0)}{\pi(1)}=2\ln\cot\frac\theta2\) this is \(\phi_t=\phi_0+\eta\Delta t\): plain gradient descent in \(\theta\) is the natural gradient in the logit, because the Fisher information of the logit is \(\pi_0\pi_1\) and \(F^{-1}\partial_\phi J=\Delta\). The time for the worse action to fall to \(\pi_\ast\) is

\[ T_\theta=\frac{\ln\frac{1-\pi_\ast}{\pi_\ast}-\phi_0}{\eta\Delta},\qquad T_{\text{flat logit}}=\frac{F(\phi_\ast)-F(\phi_0)}{\eta\Delta},\quad F(\phi)=2\sinh\phi+2\phi, \]

and \(T_\theta\propto\ln(1/\pi_\ast)\) versus \(T_{\text{flat logit}}\propto1/\pi_\ast\). The difference is not an intrinsic quantum advantage: it is the choice of the metric (of a preconditioner). The natural gradient in the logit gives the same curve as the plain one in \(\theta\).

The pole. The REINFORCE estimator (Williams 1992) \(g=r_a\,\partial_\theta\log\pi(a)\) is unbiased, with

\[ \mathbb E[g]=-\frac{\Delta\sin\theta}{2},\qquad \mathbb E[g^2]=r_0^2\sin^2\tfrac\theta2+r_1^2\cos^2\tfrac\theta2 , \]

so near a pole the mean vanishes as \(\sin\theta\) and the variance stays at \(r_1^2\) (or \(r_0^2\)): the episodes needed to resolve the sign, \(z^2\mathrm{Var}(g)/\mathbb E[g]^2\), diverge as \(1/\sin^2\theta\). And at \(\theta=0\) or \(\pi\) the gradient is exactly zero: an agent that reaches a bad pole does not leave it. The way out is an exploration floor: \(\theta\in[\theta_{\min},\pi-\theta_{\min}]\) with \(\theta_{\min}=2\arcsin\sqrt{p_{\min}}\) guarantees \(\pi(a)\ge p_{\min}\).

The decision reading. Before acting, the qang measures say how decided the policy is: \(qg_Z\) and its reading \(m_Z\) in m-qg, the entropy \(qg_S=H(p_0)\) in bits, \(qg_{SNR}=|\cot\theta|\) and the shots for the sign of \(qg_Z\) to be \(z\) deviations away, \((z/qg_{SNR})^2\) (Ch. 1). At the equator \(qg_S=1\) bit and the sign cannot be decided with any number of shots.

Tool orchestration: shot allocation. If an agent queries \(K\) readings \(qg_{Z,i}\) (one per tool or subroutine) with a budget \(B\), the sum of variances is \(\sum_i\sin^2\theta_i/n_i\), and with \(\sum n_i=B\) the minimum is \(n_i\propto\sin\theta_i\):

\[ \frac{V_{\text{uniform}}}{V_{\text{optimal}}}=\frac{K\sum_i\sin^2\theta_i}{\big(\sum_i\sin\theta_i\big)^2}\ \ge1\quad\text{(Cauchy–Schwarz)}, \]

with equality only if all the \(\sin\theta_i\) coincide. What qang contributes is that \(\sin\theta_i=\sqrt{1-qg_{Z,i}^2}\) is known from a pilot reading.

What is not there. A one-qubit policy has the same distribution as a classical coin of bias \(p_0\): quantum hardware changes neither the policy nor its learning. The learning advantages documented for quantum policies (Jerbi et al. 2021; Dunjko et al. 2016; Skolik et al. 2022) refer to models with several qubits and entanglement, or to quantum access to the environment, and this chapter neither reproduces nor claims them.

44.3 Hybrid architecture and integration with qang v0.16

 environment ──obs──► [PERCEPTION: state qubit(s) → qg_Z, qg_X]            (Qiskit)
                         │ readings with intervals (qang.probability, qang.statistics)
                         ▼
              [classical REASONING]  ◄── qang.qagent: decision_report, time_to_wrong_probability,
                         │                 episodes_for_gradient_sign, neyman_allocation
                         │ plan: shots per tool, step η, floor p_min
                         ▼
              [tool ORCHESTRATOR]  (deterministic planner; an LLM can take this place [@yao2023react])
                         │ calls: simulator/QPU · Grover (Ch. 43) · qg_Z estimator
                         ▼
              [ACTION: one shot of the policy qubit] ──► environment ──reward R──┐
                         ▲                                                       │
                         └──────── REINFORCE update in θ (with floor) ◄──────────┘

The quantum part happens in two boxes, perception (optional) and action; reasoning, the orchestrator and the update are classical. What qang adds to the reasoning is a pre-check: how much the policy trusts its action, how many steps the flow predicts, how many episodes the gradient sign costs and how to split the shots.

44.4 Practical implementation (qang + Qiskit)

import numpy as np
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.primitives import StatevectorSampler          # exact Qiskit 2.x simulator
from qang.qagent import (decision_report, exploration_floor, score, time_to_wrong_probability,
                         neyman_allocation, uniform_to_neyman_ratio)

MEANS, ETA, P_FLOOR, STEPS = (0.7, 0.3), 0.05, 0.01, 400    # Bernoulli environment and agent parameters
LO = exploration_floor(P_FLOOR)                              # θ_min = 0.2003 rad
sampler = StatevectorSampler(seed=44)
rng = np.random.default_rng(44)

# 1. Prior REASONING: what the flow predicts and how decided the policy is
print(round(time_to_wrong_probability(np.pi / 2, ETA, MEANS[0] - MEANS[1], 0.05), 1))   # 147.2 steps for π(worse) ≤ 5 %
d = decision_report(0.4)
print(round(d["qg_Z"], 3), round(d["m_Z"]), round(d["qg_S"], 3), d["shots_for_sign"])    # 0.921 921 0.24 2.0

# 2. ACTION: one shot of the policy qubit
def act(theta):
    qc = QuantumCircuit(1, 1)
    qc.ry(theta, 0)
    qc.measure(0, 0)
    counts = sampler.run([qc], shots=1).result()[0].data.c.get_counts()
    return int(next(iter(counts)))                           # 0 or 1

# 3. Perception-reasoning-action loop: REINFORCE in θ with an exploration floor
theta = np.pi / 2
for _ in range(STEPS):
    a = act(theta)
    r = float(rng.random() < MEANS[a])                       # Bernoulli reward from the environment
    theta = float(np.clip(theta + ETA * r * score(a, theta), LO, np.pi - LO))
print(round(float(np.sin(theta / 2) ** 2), 3))               # π(worse action): of the order of 0.01–0.02 (floor 0.01)

# 4. Orchestration: split B shots among K=4 pilot readings
thetas = [0.2, 1.0, np.pi / 2, 2.9]
print(np.round(neyman_allocation(thetas, 4000)).astype(int).tolist(), round(uniform_to_neyman_ratio(thetas), 3))
# [349, 1477, 1755, 420] 1.389

Interpretation.

  • The values of steps 1 and 4 are deterministic and reproducible. The flow predicts 147.2 steps to bring the worse action down to \(5\%\) with \(\eta=0.05\) and \(\Delta=0.4\); the policy at \(\theta=0.4\) reads \(qg_Z=0.921\) (921 m-qg), \(qg_S=0.240\) bits and needs 2 shots to fix the sign at \(3\sigma\). The optimal allocation for \(\theta=(0.2,\,1.0,\,\pi/2,\,2.9)\) gives \((349,\,1477,\,1755,\,420)\) and lowers the sum of variances by \(1.389\) with respect to the uniform split.
  • Step 3 is stochastic. With the same loop in NumPy (tools/verify_cap44.py uses run_bandit, which draws the action with the same probability \(\cos^2(\theta/2)\)), 300 runs of 400 steps give a mean \(\pi(\text{worse})\) of \(0.020\) and a median of \(0.010\) (the floor). A single run with Qiskit varies; the printed value is an order of magnitude, not a guarantee.
  • Qiskit was not installed in the environment where the chapter was written: the block was not executed, and its step-3 output remains to be checked in the reader’s environment. The identities behind it are verified with NumPy sampling.
  • Agreement with the flow (verified): with \(\eta=0.02\) and 100 steps, 400 Bernoulli runs give \(\pi(\text{worse})=0.316\) against \(0.310\) predicted by the closed flow.

44.5 Use cases, limitations (NISQ versus simulators) and future directions

Use cases with support.

agent decision how qang.qagent helps support
how many steps learning predicts before running it closed-form flow time in \(\theta\) time_to_wrong_probability, flow_theta
whether the policy already decides its action \(qg_Z\), \(qg_S\), \(qg_{SNR}\), shots for the sign decision_report
how many episodes the gradient sign costs (and whether there is a pole) \(z^2\mathrm{Var}(g)/\mathbb E[g]^2\) episodes_for_gradient_sign
how to avoid the bad pole exploration floor \(\theta_{\min}\) exploration_floor, run_bandit
how to split the shots among quantum tools \(n_i\propto\sin\theta_i\) neyman_allocation, uniform_to_neyman_ratio

Limitations. (i) Latency: an action is one shot, and the time per shot plus the queue of a cloud service make an interactive loop of hundreds of steps on a QPU unviable; on a simulator there is no problem, but there is no advantage either. (ii) Classical equivalence: a one-qubit policy is a biased coin; the benefit of the \(\theta\) parametrization is a choice of metric that a classical natural gradient reproduces. (iii) Several qubits: the advantages in the literature require entanglement or quantum access to the environment; qang has no verified result there, and THEORY_CORE.md (§2) recalls that reading \(qg_Z\) per qubit loses against the string histogram (Ch. 3). (iv) Noise: the model is ideal; with readout noise the affine readout must be calibrated (Ch. 30) before using \(qg_Z\) as a policy.

Future directions, without result. Orchestration by a language model: a ReAct-type agent (Yao et al. 2023) could call the functions of qang.qagent as tools; there is no experiment in this book, unverified. Multi-qubit policies and quantum Q-learning (Skolik et al. 2022): the qang metric for several qubits (Ch. 5, local radius) is a starting point, unverified. Natural gradient with a floor: test with a pre-registered experiment whether the optimal exploration floor \(p_{\min}\) depends on \(\Delta\).

44.6 Without qang and with qang

action without qang with qang difference
forecast the learning speed run and look at the curve \(T_\theta=(\ln\frac{1-\pi_\ast}{\pi_\ast}-\phi_0)/(\eta\Delta)\) prior calculation; no gain in learning
choose the parametrization logit with flat gradient (\(\propto1/\pi_\ast\)) angle \(\theta\) (\(\propto\ln(1/\pi_\ast)\)) choice of metric: the classical natural gradient gives the same
detect the pole the agent stops learning zero gradient, \(1/\sin^2\theta\) episodes, floor \(\theta_{\min}\) exact diagnosis
know whether the policy already decides sample until it shows \(qg_Z\), \(qg_{SNR}\), shots for the sign prior check
split shots among \(K\) tools uniform \(n_i\propto\sin\theta_i\) up to \(K\sum\sin^2/(\sum\sin)^2\) less variance (1.389 in the example)

Balance. qang does not make the agent smarter: it gives exact accounts of its policy (how much it trusts, how long it takes to learn, where it gets stuck, how to spend the shots). A one-qubit policy is a biased coin, and the quantum learning advantage stays outside what this book verifies. There is no measured gain.

44.7 Verification — tools/verify_cap44.py

python tools/verify_cap44.py          # NumPy + qang only; ~1 min
