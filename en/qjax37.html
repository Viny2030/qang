Chapter 37 — Nanoquantum Systems: Tunnelling, Landauer, Partition Noise, Level Occupation, Quantum-Dot Spins and NV Centres Read with the qang Equation

Updated: November 16, 2026

Rewrite of the nanoquantum chapter on the basis of Ch. 1–36. Module: qang.qnano (NumPy; binomial noise is computed with the exact distribution, no Monte Carlo, except for thermometry). Verification: tools/verify_cap37.py. Everything is calculation and simulation: no figure comes from an experiment. In the only case where qang competes as an estimator (thermometry), it loses. Abstract. Transmissions, occupations and spin populations are probabilities, so the qang equation reads them all alike: the half-angle of a tunnelling probability is the transmission amplitude modulus; Landauer’s conductance is the mean \(qg\) of the channels; partition noise is the binomial variance, stabilised by the angle (\(\sqrt N\sigma_\theta\in[1.0005,1.0100]\) for \(T\in[0.02,0.98]\)). As a thermometer, the artanh line is biased (\(-1.9\%\)) and noisier than the maximum-likelihood fit. Calculation and simulation only.

37.1 Reading rules

  1. \(p_0\) is, depending on the case, the transmission \(T\), the occupation \(f\) of a level or the population of a spin state. With \(p_0\): \(\theta=2\arccos\sqrt{p_0}\), \(qg_Z=2p_0-1\), m-qg \(=1000\,qg_Z\); 1 m-qg is always \(\Delta p_0=0.0005\).
  2. qga (1 turn = 1000 qga) appears only as the scale of the angle: \(\sigma_\theta=1/\sqrt N\) is equivalent to \(159.15/\sqrt N\) qga.
  3. Each claim carries its function in qang.qnano; the contrast with the usual form goes alongside, and it is stated when qang loses.

37.2 Tunnelling: the half-angle is the amplitude

Rectangular barrier of 1 eV, electron of 0.5 eV (\(\kappa=3.623\) nm⁻¹), exact formula (barrier_T):

width \(T\) reading \(\theta_T\)
0.2 nm 0.616 +231.9 m-qg 76.6°
0.5 nm 0.101 −797.3 m-qg 142.9°
1.0 nm \(2.85\cdot10^{-3}\) −994.3 m-qg 173.9°
1.5 nm \(7.62\cdot10^{-5}\) −999.8 m-qg 179.0°

\(\cos(\theta_T/2)=\sqrt T=|t|\) to \(10^{-15}\): the half-angle is the modulus of the transmission amplitude. Without qang, \(\ln T\) is plotted, with slope \(-2\kappa\) (\(-7.242\) measured, \(-7.245\) expected). With qang, the distance to the pole \(\pi-\theta_T\approx2|t|\) falls with slope \(-\kappa\) (\(-3.622\) versus \(-3.623\) nm⁻¹): the amplitude instead of the probability, the same information.

37.3 Landauer: one channel, one reading

\(G/G_0=\sum_nT_n=N/2+\tfrac12\sum_n qg_n\), with the saddle-point model \(T_n=1/(1+e^{-2\pi(V-n-1/2)/w})\) (\(w=0.45\)): identity to \(4.4\cdot10^{-16}\) (conductance_from_qg). The plateaus are at \(V=2,3,4\) with \(G/G_0=1,2,3\); each step is a channel crossing the equator. Conductance alone gives \(\sum T_n\); with shot noise \(S=\sum T_n(1-T_n)\) two simultaneously open channels are recovered (recover_two_channels): from \(G=1.3747\), \(S=0.2682\) come exactly \(T=0.9716\) and \(0.4031\). It is the usual technique; in qg, conductance reads the mean \(qg\) and noise the mean \(qg^2\).

37.4 Partition noise: the binomial variance, stabilized

\(T(1-T)=(1-qg^2)/4=\sin^2\theta/4\). With \(N=1000\) electrons per window and the exact binomial distribution (theta_std_sqrtN):

\(T\) 0.02 0.05 0.2 0.5 0.8 0.95 0.98
\(\sqrt N\,\sigma(\hat T)\) 0.140 0.218 0.400 0.500 0.400 0.218 0.140
\(\sqrt N\,\sigma(\hat\theta)\) 1.0100 1.0038 1.0009 1.0005 1.0009 1.0038 1.0100

The error bar of \(\hat T\) depends on what is measured and misleads near the poles; that of \(\hat\theta\) is the same over the whole range. It is the arcsine transformation, known in statistics: qang does not invent it; it contributes that the stabilized angle is the natural coordinate of the state.

37.5 Coulomb blockade: level occupation

\(qg_Z=2f-1=-\tanh(x/2)\), with \(f=1/(1+e^x)\), \(x=\varepsilon/k_BT\) (to \(10^{-15}\)): the same thermal state as \(qg_Z=\tanh(\beta h)\) of Ch. 11 (ex-8), with the sign of the convention. As a thermometer (25 levels, 200 readings per level, 400 repetitions, \(k_BT=1\)):

method estimated \(k_BT\) (seed 34) 4 seeds
Fermi fit by maximum likelihood (without qang) \(1.0000\pm0.0275\) 0.9997–1.0008
line of \(\operatorname{artanh}(qg_Z)\) through the origin (with qang) \(0.9808\pm0.0366\) 0.9805–0.9813

qang loses. The line is closed-form but has a bias of \(-1.9\%\) and a \(1.3\times\) larger spread: \(\operatorname{artanh}\) amplifies noise near \(\pm1\) and saturated points must be discarded. The previous version of the chapter gave \(+2.9\%\) with another recipe (line with intercept); the sign and size of the bias depend on the recipe, not the result: no artanh line beats the Fermi fit. The qg reading serves to see the thermometer, not to estimate with it.

37.6 Spin qubits in quantum dots

Charge-sensor readout has errors \(e_0=P(1\mid0)\), \(e_1=P(0\mid1)\), and the rule of Ch. 30 (ex-27) in m-qg is \(m'=(1-e_0-e_1)m+1000(e_1-e_0)\) (readout_affine). With \(e_0=0.02\), \(e_1=0.08\) (illustrative values): \(+1000\to960\) and \(-1000\to-840\) m-qg, that is, amplitude 900 m-qg and shift +60 m-qg (each percentage point of readout error costs 10 m-qg of visibility); correcting amplifies noise by \(1/(1-e_0-e_1)=1.11\times\). It is the standard correction written linearly.

37.7 NV centres: contrast as a budget

With \(n\sim\text{Poisson}(N_b[1-C(1-p_0)])\), the reading of \(p_0\) in m-qg has (nv_sigma_mqg)

\[\sigma_m=\frac{2000\sqrt{1-C(1-p_0)}}{C\sqrt{N_b}}\ \approx\ \frac{2000}{C\sqrt{N_b}}.\]

With \(C=0.3\) and \(N_b=444\,444\): the approximation gives 10.00 m-qg and the exact formula 9.22 m-qg; for exactly 10 m-qg one needs 377 778 photons (nv_photons_for). The contrast \(C=0.3\) costs ≈\(1/C^2=11.1\times\) the photons of \(C=1\). In ODMR (Lorentzian line of 1 MHz, \(C=0.2\), 61 frequencies over \(\pm3\) widths, 1.16 M photons), the Cramér–Rao bound of the center is 8.55 kHz = 305 nT with \(\gamma=28\) GHz/T (odmr_fisher_crb_hz). The line fit does not change with qang; the scale adds the prior count in a unit linear in the population.

tests/test_qnano.py

import numpy as np
from qang.qnano import barrier_T, theta_from_p0, recover_two_channels, theta_std_sqrtN, thermometry, nv_sigma_mqg, nv_photons_for
def test_tunnel_amplitude():
    T = barrier_T(1.0); assert abs(np.cos(theta_from_p0(T) / 2) - np.sqrt(T)) < 1e-15 and abs(T - 2.85e-3) < 1e-5
def test_two_channels():
    r = recover_two_channels(0.4031 + 0.9716, 0.4031 * 0.5969 + 0.9716 * 0.0284); assert abs(r[0] - 0.9716) < 1e-12
def test_arcsine_variance_stabilized(): assert all(1.0 <= theta_std_sqrtN(t, 400) < 1.02 for t in (0.05, 0.3, 0.5, 0.9))
def test_thermometry_loses():
    (ml, _), (ar, _) = thermometry(reps=60, seed=5); assert abs(ml - 1) < 0.02 and ar < 0.995
def test_nv(): assert abs(nv_sigma_mqg(0.3, nv_photons_for(10, 0.3)) - 10) < 1e-9

(The rest, test_landauer_identity_and_two_channels, test_fermi_tanh_and_thermometry_loses and test_readout_and_nv, is in tests/test_qnano.py.)
