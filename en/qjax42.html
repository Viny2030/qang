Chapter 42 — String Theory Read with qang: Exact Counting, the Level as a Z Reading, T-Duality and the Radius of a Lattice Analogue

Updated: November 16, 2026

New chapter, following Ch. 33 (free boson \(c=1\) and scope with respect to strings) without repeating it. Module: qang.qstring (NumPy; SciPy only in §42.5). Verification: tools/verify_cap42.py. Status: exact for the identities of the model (§42.2–§42.4); mixed for the radius read on a finite-size XXZ chain (§42.5); that qang measures string physics is not adopted: identities of a model and a lattice analogue are read, the theory is not proven. No hardware.

Abstract. What of string theory can be read and verified with qang? (i) Light-cone bosonic string: level degeneracies \(1,24,324,3200,25650,176256\), critical dimension \(D=26\), and the closed massless level \(576=299+276+1\). (ii) The level \(N=\sum_k k\,x_k\) is a weighted Hamming weight, hence a linear single-basis Z reading, \(N=\sum_k k(1000-m_k)/2000\) with \(m_k\) in m-qg, and its \(T_1\) filter keeps exactly \((1-\gamma)^{|x|}\); the level filter and the Hamming-weight filter have complementary blind spots. (iii) T-duality \(R\to\alpha'/R\) leaves the level-matched spectrum unchanged at six radii; at the self-dual radius the massless count rises from 576 to 676. (iv) In an XXZ ring of 20 sites, a single Z histogram gives the Luttinger parameter (the compactification radius of the lattice analogue) from the block weight variance within 1.3 % at \(\Delta=0\) and 13 % at \(\Delta=0.71\). None of this measures string physics.

42.1 Reading rules

  1. Each result enters with its function in qang.qstring and its line in tools/verify_cap42.py. Scales: \(m\text{-qg}=1000\,qg_Z\); this chapter does not use qga.
  2. What is computed is an exact identity of the model (counting, spectrum) or a Z reading of a qubit analogue. No number here is a measurement of the physical string, of \(D=26\) as the dimension of spacetime, or of the coupling.
  3. Ch. 33 already recorded that the local readings \(qg_Z\) and \(qg_S\) are flat with respect to the central charge (\(\texttt{against}\)). Here it is shown which readings do depend on the model parameter and which do not.

42.2 The light-cone bosonic string: exact counting

With \(d=24\) transverse oscillators, the degeneracy of level \(N\) is the coefficient of \(q^N\) in \(\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{-24}\) (colored_partitions): \(1,\,24,\,324,\,3200,\,25650,\,176256\) for \(N=0,\dots,5\). The zero-point energy is \(-(D-2)/24\) (zero_point_energy); the first excited level is massless only if \((D-2)/24=1\), that is \(D=26\) (critical_dimension, unique in \(3\le D<60\)). For the open string, \(\alpha' M^2=N-1\): \(-1\) at \(N=0\) (tachyon) and \(0\) at \(N=1\). In the closed string, level \(N=\tilde N=1\) has \(24\times24=576\) states, which decompose into \(299\) (graviton, symmetric traceless) \(+\,276\) (Kalb–Ramond, antisymmetric) \(+\,1\) (dilaton) (closed_massless_decomposition).

Cost in qubits to label the states of a level (only to size it, the spectrum is not encoded): \(\lceil\log_2 d_N\rceil=5,\,9,\,12,\,15,\,18\) for \(N=1,\dots,5\).

42.3 The level \(N\) as a linear Z reading and its filter under \(T_1\)

With one qubit per mode (\(k\)-th mode \(=\) qubit \(k-1\), occupation 0/1: this is the fermionic or “distinct parts” case, not the 24 bosonic oscillators), the level is the weighted weight \(W(x)=\sum_k k\,x_k\). It is linear in the readings: \(\langle N\rangle=\sum_k k\,(1000-m_k)/2000\), with \(m_k\) the \(qg_Z\) reading of qubit \(k-1\) in m-qg (level_from_z, level_from_readings; difference with the histogram \(7\cdot10^{-15}\)). The size of the level-\(N\) sector with \(n=10\) modes is the number of partitions of \(N\) into distinct parts: \(1,1,1,2,2,3,4,5,6,8,10\) (level_sector_size, distinct_partitions).

Budget. With \(n=6\) and uniform \(p\), \(\mathrm{Var}\,N=22.75\); for \(\sigma_N=0.1\) at 95 % one needs \(8740\) Z shots (level_shots), with no additional settings.

Level filter under \(T_1\). Any decay of an occupied mode lowers the level, so the level filter always detects it; the retention is exactly \((1-\gamma)^{|x|}\) (level_filter_damped). For the level-14 state (modes 1, 3, 4, 6): \(\gamma=0.1\) retains \(0.6561=(0.9)^4\); \(\gamma=0.3\) retains \(0.2401\) and the raw mean level is \(9.80\) versus \(14\).

Two complementary blind spots (level_vs_weight_blindness): the exchange \(\{3\}\leftrightarrow\{1,2\}\) conserves the level (3) and changes the weight (\(1\to2\)): the level filter does not see it. The move \(\{1\}\to\{2\}\) conserves the weight and changes the level (\(1\to2\)): the weight filter of Ch. 34 does not see it. Each filter needs to know which quantity the circuit conserves.

42.4 T-duality of the compactified boson

With \(p_{L,R}=n/R\pm wR/\alpha'\) and \((h,\bar h)=(\alpha' p_L^2/4+N-1,\ \alpha' p_R^2/4+\tilde N-1)\), the level matching is \(nw+N-\tilde N=0\) (conformal_weights, closed_spectrum). The spectrum with \(h,\bar h\le2\) and 24 oscillators per side is identical at \(R\) and at \(\alpha'/R\) (exchange \(n\leftrightarrow w\)): checked at \(R=0.5,\,0.7,\,1,\,1.3,\,2,\,3\) (t_dual_equal). Massless states (\(h=\bar h=0\), massless_states): \(R=0.7\) and \(1.3\): \(576\); \(R=0.5\) and \(2\): \(580\); \(R=3\): \(578\); self-dual radius \(R=\sqrt{\alpha'}\): \(676=576+4\cdot24+4\), with \(4\cdot24=96\) vector states at \((n,w)=(\pm1,\pm1)\) (massless_breakdown) and \(4\) scalars at \((\pm2,0),(0,\pm2)\). The enlargement from \(576\) to \(676\) is the extended symmetry of the self-dual radius.

42.5 The radius, read with a single Z basis: an XXZ chain

The XXZ chain \(H=\sum_i S^x_iS^x_{i+1}+S^y_iS^y_{i+1}+\Delta S^z_iS^z_{i+1}\) is the lattice analogue of a compactified boson; its Luttinger parameter \(K=\pi/(2(\pi-\arccos\Delta))\) (luttinger_k_exact; \(K=1\) at \(\Delta=0\), \(K=1/2\) at \(\Delta=1\)) fixes the radius, with an identification \(K\leftrightarrow R^2/\alpha'\) that depends on convention. In the ring, the variance of the Hamming weight of a block of \(\ell\) qubits, read from a single Z histogram, grows as \((K/\pi^2)\ln[(L/\pi)\sin(\pi\ell/L)]\) (block_weight_variance, luttinger_k_from_variance; fit with even \(\ell\ge4\), because odd \(\ell\) oscillate).

20-site ring, \(S^z=0\) sector (xxz_ground_distribution, SciPy):

\(\Delta\) exact \(K\) \(\hat K\) (one Z basis) error
\(0\) \(1.000\) \(1.013\) \(1.3\%\)
\(-0.5\) \(1.500\) \(1.514\) \(0.9\%\)
\(0.5\) \(0.750\) \(0.811\) \(8.2\%\)
\(0.7071\) \(0.667\) \(0.756\) \(13.4\%\)

The error grows toward \(\Delta>0\) because of logarithmic corrections near \(\Delta=1\): the status is mixed. In the continuum, the exponents of the staggered correlations are \(2K\) (ZZ) and \(1/(2K)\) (XX): their product is \(1\) and T-duality \(K\to1/K\) exchanges them (dual_exponents). The pair \(K=3/2\) (\(\Delta=-0.5\)) and \(K=2/3\) (\(\Delta=\cos\pi/4\)) gives a read product of \(1.145\) (error \(14.5\%\), that of the \(\Delta>0\) branch): the lattice chain is not T-dual to itself, only its continuum limit is.

42.6 What it does not measure

  • It does not measure \(D=26\) as the dimension of spacetime, nor the physical mass spectrum, nor the coupling: they are identities of a model.
  • The level \(W(x)\) with 0/1 occupation is not the spectrum of the 24 bosonic oscillators; it is the fermionic case. The degeneracies \(d_N\) are counted, not encoded in qubits.
  • The radius of the XXZ chain is that of a finite-size lattice analogue; the identification with \(R/\sqrt{\alpha'}\) depends on convention.
  • The local readings \(qg_Z\) and \(qg_S\) still do not measure the central charge (Ch. 33, against); the block variance of §42.5 does depend on \(K\) because it uses the joint histogram of several qubits.
