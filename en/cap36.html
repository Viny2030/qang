36  Decision-making in quantum environments: from model-free to model-aware learning

Status: proposed chapter (new Part XIV). Source: M. Bilkis, Decision-making in quantum environments: from model-free to model-aware learning of quantum controls, PhD thesis, UAB (2023), and the talk “Three quantum problems”. Check: python tools/verify_cap36.py (28 checks; NumPy + qang 0.14.0 only). Units: angles are in qga (1 turn = 1000 qga); readings are in qg_Z or in m-qg \(= 1000\,qg_Z\). The two are not interchangeable.

36.1 36.1 The axis: how much the agent knows

A classical agent acts on a quantum system, receives a measurement outcome and chooses the next action. The three problems of the talk are ordered by what the agent knows about the system:

Communications (36.2) NISQ computing (36.3) Sensing (36.4)
Knowledge of the model none (model-free) partial (the circuit’s form, not the noise) complete (model-aware)
Action displacement \(\beta\) ansatz parameters and structure stop or keep measuring; chosen hypothesis
Data click / no click expectation values with shot noise photocurrent \(dy_t\)
Tool Q-learning, bandits parameter shift, VAns quantum filter, likelihood ratio
What qang adds linear reading of the receiver; effect size \(h\) minimal area; sector filter under \(T_1\) \(h\) for the shot budget; gain \(= 1/I_F\)

The talk goes through them in the opposite order (sensing, NISQ, communications); this chapter follows the thesis, from less model to more.

What this chapter does not claim. None of the three sections shows a new qang advantage measured on hardware. The identities are exact, two savings come from known statistics (Wald) or from knowing the model, and the only qang gain quoted (36.3.3) is the one already recorded in §112.

36.2 36.2 Communications: telling \(|{+}\alpha\rangle\) from \(|{-}\alpha\rangle\)

36.2.1 36.2.1 Physical picture

A strongly attenuated laser pulse arrives as a coherent state \(|\pm\alpha\rangle\) with \(|\alpha|^2 \lesssim 1\) photons. The two states are not orthogonal, \(c = \langle -\alpha|\alpha\rangle = e^{-2\alpha^2}\), so no measurement separates them without error. Two pure states span a two-dimensional subspace: the problem is a qubit problem, and the separation between the hypotheses is an angle.

36.2.2 36.2.2 Formalism

On the Bloch sphere of that subspace the two states are \(\Delta\) apart, with \(\cos(\Delta/2) = c\). The geodesic distance is \(qg_L = \Delta/2 = \arccos c\) and the Helstrom bound reads

\[P_H = \tfrac12\left(1 + \sqrt{1 - c^2}\right) = \tfrac12\left(1 + \sin qg_L\right), \qquad 2\sqrt{P_H(1-P_H)} = c .\]

The generalized Kennedy receiver displaces the pulse by \(\beta\) and uses an on/off detector; with no click it decides \(-\alpha\):

\[P_s(\beta) = \tfrac12\left[1 - e^{-(\alpha+\beta)^2} + e^{-(\beta-\alpha)^2}\right].\]

The detector is one binary variable per hypothesis, with \(p_0^{\pm} = e^{-(\beta\pm\alpha)^2}\) and reading \(qg_Z^{\pm} = 2p_0^{\pm} - 1\). Then

\[P_s(\beta) = \tfrac12 + \tfrac14\left(qg_Z^{-} - qg_Z^{+}\right),\]

linear in the two readings: a difference of \(1\ \text{m-qg}\) is \(2.5\cdot 10^{-4}\) of success probability.

The real saving of this section comes from the model, not from the unit:

Agent (2000 episodes, 200 seeds) What it knows Cumulative regret
Model-free: UCB1 over 21 values of \(\beta\) nothing; reward = correct guess \(136.7 \pm 0.5\)
Model-aware: maximum likelihood of \(\alpha\), plays \(\beta^{*}(\hat\alpha)\) the law \(p_0 = e^{-(\beta\pm\alpha)^2}\) \(0.40 \pm 0.03\)

The aware agent turns every click into information on one parameter, \(\alpha^2 = -\ln\frac{1+qg_Z}{2}\) at \(\beta = 0\); the agnostic one spreads its episodes over 21 arms. The other side is the one the talk gives: if the channel cannot be modelled or changes abruptly, the law no longer holds and only the model-free agent keeps learning. That case (an abrupt change of environment) is not simulated here.

h = proportions.cohens_h(0.8091, 0.7364)               # 0.1741
n_per_arm = math.ceil(2 * (proportions.z_value(0.95) / h) ** 2)   # 254

36.3 36.3 NISQ computing: gradients, plateaus and variable structure

36.3.1 36.3.1 What the shift rule is for

On a simulator the state is known and automatic differentiation gives any derivative. On a device there are only expectation values estimated from shots. For a gate \(e^{-i\theta P/2}\) the cost is \(f(\theta) = a + b\cos\theta + c\sin\theta\) and

\[\partial_\theta f = \tfrac12\left[f(\theta + \tfrac\pi2) - f(\theta - \tfrac\pi2)\right],\]

exact and evaluated with the same circuit. Two evaluations per parameter are needed in any coordinate: fisher.indistinguishable_pair builds two costs with the same value at \(\theta + s\) and different derivatives.

Decision Without qang With qang Status
Evaluations per parameter 2 2 no gain (§159)
Descent in \(qg_Z\) instead of \(\theta\) converges diverges at the poles against
Barren plateaus after moving to qga the same the same: every derivative is multiplied by \(2\pi/1000\) no gain

36.3.2 36.3.2 VAns and the minimal area

VAns inserts blocks that resolve the identity, optimizes, simplifies, and accepts or rejects. After optimization the angles may lie outside the principal interval. The minimal-area rule (qang.pulse_area, §160) moves each rotation to its shortest representative (\(R_X\): period 1000 qga; \(R_{ZZ}\): 500 qga with virtual \(Z\)) and removes those left at zero; the unitary does not change.

Constructed example, 9 gates (\(R_X\): \(2\pi + 0.10\), \(-3.50\), \(2\pi\), \(4.00\), \(0.25\); \(R_{ZZ}\): \(\pi + 0.05\), \(2.90\), \(\pi\), \(-1.70\) rad):

Without the rule With minimal_area_angle
Total area 31.35 rad = 4989 qga 7.15 rad = 1138 qga
Fractional gates 9 7

This is a synthetic example, not a circuit trained by VAns: it fixes the rule, not a measured gain. Whether adding it as a VAns simplification step improves VAns results is unverified.

36.3.3 36.3.3 Noise-induced plateaus and the sector filter

Under noise the gradient shrinks with depth and the number of shots needed to resolve it grows exponentially. In an ansatz that conserves the Hamming weight, under equal \(T_1\) on all qubits, the raw reading shrinks the gradient by \(K = (1-\gamma)^{w\,d}\) and the filtered reading returns the noiseless gradient (§112), at the price of keeping a fraction \(K\) of the shots:

Depth \(d\) (\(\gamma = 0.08\), \(w = 1\)) \(K\) = qml.kept_fraction Shots \(\times\,1/K\)
3 0.779 1.28
6 0.606 1.65
12 0.368 2.72
24 0.135 7.40

The filter does not remove the plateau: it trades an exponentially small gradient for a post-selection cost exponential in \(w\,d\). In §112 it needed fewer shots in 10 of 12 cells and more when \(K \lesssim 0.15\). It does not correct dephasing or in-sector errors.

36.4 36.4 Sensing: deciding on a noisy trajectory

36.4.1 36.4.1 Physical picture

A continuously monitored sensor delivers a photocurrent: signal plus white noise. The state conditioned on that signal follows a stochastic equation. The question is which model generated the data, and when there is enough evidence to stop.

36.4.2 36.4.2 Formalism for one qubit

With continuous measurement of \(\sigma_z\), rate \(\kappa\) and efficiency \(\eta\), and \(k = \sqrt{\eta\kappa}\):

\[d\rho = \kappa\,\mathcal D[\sigma_z]\rho\,dt + k\,\mathcal H[\sigma_z]\rho\,dW, \qquad dy = 2k\,qg_Z\,dt + dW ,\]

\[d\,qg_Z = 2k\left(1 - qg_Z^2\right)dW = \frac{2k}{I_F(qg_Z)}\,dW .\]

The innovation gain is the inverse of the Fisher information of the reading: it vanishes at the poles, where nothing is left to learn. In the angle, by Itô, \(d\theta = -2k\sin\theta\,dW - k^2\sin 2\theta\,dt\). For two hypotheses with signals \(s_i = 2k\,qg_Z^{(i)}\), each filtered with its own model, the log-likelihood ratio is

\[d\ell = (s_1 - s_0)\left[dy - \tfrac12(s_1 + s_0)\,dt\right],\]

and the sequential test stops when \(\ell\) leaves \(\left[\ln\frac{\beta}{1-\alpha},\ \ln\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha}\right]\).

Numerical check (4000 trajectories, \(t = 4/k^2\), \(qg_Z(0) = 0.3\)): the mean is conserved, \(0.3045 \pm 0.0151\), and 65.2% collapse to \(|0\rangle\) (expected \(p_0 = 0.65\)).

36.4.3 36.4.3 Stopping in time: sequential test against fixed \(N\)

In shots: \(H_0: qg_Z = 0\) against \(H_1: qg_Z = 0.2\) (200 m-qg), 1% errors.

Fixed \(N\) Sequential test (Wald)
Shots 534 \(= \lceil (2z/h)^2\rceil\), \(h = 0.2014\) rad \(= 32.0\) qga 225 (\(H_0\)) and 228 (\(H_1\)) on average; theory 221 and 224
Measured errors 1% nominal 0.91% and 0.94%
Saving 57%

The saving belongs to Wald’s sequential test (1945), not to qang: qang gives the effect size \(h\) that fixes the reference \(N\). qang 0.14.0 has no sequential-test function; the one in the script is local.

36.4.4 36.4.4 What is left out

The Kalman filter of the talk holds for Gaussian continuous-variable systems (optomechanical cavities, atomic sensors in that regime). \(qg_Z\) is a qubit unit and does not apply to those quadratures: not adopted. The recurrent network that learns the equation of the external signal (qmon_sindy) is not reformulated here either.

36.6 36.6 Summary

  • Two coherent states are a qubit: Helstrom is \(\tfrac12(1+\sin qg_L)\) and the displacement receiver is read linearly in \(qg_Z\).
  • Knowing the model lowers the regret from 136.7 to 0.40 over 2000 episodes; the unit does not change the number of episodes (254 against 253).
  • In VQAs a change of unit gives no shortcut for the gradient or against plateaus; there is an exact area rule, and a filter that returns the noiseless gradient under \(T_1\) at a cost of \(1/K\) shots.
  • In sensing, the gain of the quantum filter is \(1/I_F(qg_Z)\) and the sequential test saves 57% of the shots in the example.
