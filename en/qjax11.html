Chapter 11 — Metrology in qga: Ramsey Operating Point, GHZ under Dephasing, tanh Thermometry and Clock Comparison

Analysis and update date: November 22, 2026

Rewrite of the old Ch. 8 on the basis of Ch. 1–9 (general Fisher information now lives in Ch. 9; here only applications). Module: qang.qmetro (JAX, jax<0.9; aligned with qang.fisher, qang.clock_comparison, examples §25, §36, §58, §126). Verification: tools/verify_cap11.py.

11.1 Ramsey operating point in the readout coordinate

After the second \(\pi/2\) pulse the reading is \(qg_Z=C\cos\phi\), with \(C=e^{-T/T_2}\) the contrast and \(\phi=\omega T\). Fisher information per shot (Ch. 9): \[I_F(\phi)=\frac{C^2\sin^2\phi}{1-C^2\cos^2\phi}\ \le\ C^2,\qquad \text{equality at }\phi=\tfrac\pi2\iff qg_Z=0\ (250\text{ qga}).\] For \(C=1\) it is uniform (\(I_F\equiv1\), \(\sigma_{\text{qga}}=159.15/\sqrt N\)); for \(C<1\) the equator advantage is strict: operating at \(\phi=0.2\) rad loses 90 % (\(C=0.8\)) and 95 % (\(C=0.5\)) of the information. qang rule: fix the operating point by \(qg_Z=0\) read in m-qg, not by the nominal \(\phi\) (exact). Optimal time per unit time: \(\max_T T e^{-2T/T_2}\Rightarrow T^\star=T_2/2\). With finite contrast the equator (\(qg_Z=0\)) is the unique optimum; the operating point is set by the readout value, not by the nominal phase.

For \(C=1\) the expression is identically \(1\): the equator gains no local information and verify_cap11.py reports loss -0%; for \(C<1\) the maximum is strict at \(qg_Z=0\), where \(|dqg_Z/d\phi|=C\) is maximal. The statement is local: it optimizes the Fisher information for a known \(\phi\); with finite priors or Bayesian risk the optimum can shift (variational Ramsey/clock optimization). The optimal time \(T^\star=T_2/2\) assumes Markovian dephasing with \(C=e^{-T/T_2}\) and the per-unit-time metric \(I_F(\omega)/T=T e^{-2T/T_2}\) at the equator; with dead time, non-Markovian laser noise or a broad-prior Bayesian estimator, \(T^\star\) is not given by this closed form.

Sources for this section. S. F. Huelga et al., “Improvement of Frequency Standards with Quantum Entanglement”, Phys. Rev. Lett. 79, 3865 (1997), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.79.3865, https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9707014; “Quantum Fisher information measurement and verification of the quantum Cramér–Rao bound in a solid-state qubit”, npj Quantum Information (2022), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-022-00547-x; R. Kaubruegger et al., “Quantum Variational Optimization of Ramsey Interferometry and Atomic Clocks”, Phys. Rev. X 11, 041045 (2021), https://journals.aps.org/prx/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevX.11.041045.

11.2 GHZ versus product under Markovian dephasing: no gain

Parity \(\langle X^{\otimes n}\rangle=e^{-nT/T_2}\cos n\phi\): \[\frac{I_F^{\text{GHZ}}(\omega)}{T}=n^2Te^{-2nT/T_2},\qquad \frac{I_F^{\text{prod}}(\omega)}{T}=nTe^{-2T/T_2},\qquad \max_T:\ \frac{nT_2}{2e}\ \text{in both cases}\ (T^\star_{\text{GHZ}}=\tfrac{T_2}{2n},\ T^\star_{\text{prod}}=\tfrac{T_2}{2}).\] Verified by optimal_T (descent in \(\log T\) with jax.grad) for \(n=2,5,10\) to \(10^{-9}\). At fixed \(T=T_2/2\) the \(n=5\) GHZ yields \(0.092\times\); it wins only if \(T<T_2\ln n/(2(n-1))\). Status no gain (Huelga et al. 1997 reproduced in qg coordinates); consistent with THEORY_CORE §2 (do not claim a Heisenberg advantage under Markovian noise).

The Fisher information computed is that of the parity measurement \(\langle X^{\otimes n}\rangle=e^{-nT/T_2}\cos(n\phi)\), not the QCRB of an arbitrary measurement. The no gain is therefore bounded to independent Markovian dephasing and parity readout: Degen et al. and the measurement of GHZ decoherence in superconducting qubits support the mechanism that the decoherence rate grows linearly with \(n\) and offsets the phase gain. It must not be generalized to any noise: with spontaneous decay and a correlated measurement with a nonlinear estimator, Kielinski et al. show that GHZ/heralded-uGHZ protocols can beat the SQL (1.8 dB and 2.25 dB); ordinary parity does not saturate the QCRB in that model.

Sources for this section. C. L. Degen, F. Reinhard and P. Cappellaro, “Quantum sensing”, Rev. Mod. Phys. 89, 035002 (2017), https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/124553/RevModPhys.89.035002.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2; A. Ozaeta and P. L. McMahon, “Decoherence of up to 8-qubit entangled states in a 16-qubit superconducting quantum processor” (2018), https://arxiv.org/html/1712.07080v2; T. Kielinski, P. O. Schmidt and K. Hammerer, “GHZ protocols enhance frequency metrology despite spontaneous decay”, Sci. Adv. 10, eadr1439 (2024), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11639174.

11.3 One-qubit thermometry: qg_Z=tanh(x/2)

Gibbs state, \(x=\hbar\omega/k_BT\): \(qg_Z=\tanh(x/2)\) (§25). Fisher information per shot: \[I_F(x)=\frac{(\partial_xqg_Z)^2}{1-qg_Z^2}=\tfrac14\operatorname{sech}^2\tfrac x2,\qquad I_F(\ln T)=x^2I_F(x)\ \Rightarrow\ x^\star=2.399,\ qg_Z^\star=0.834\ (834\text{ m-qg}),\ I_F^\star=0.439 .\] The optimal sensor of relative temperature is therefore calibrated at a reading of \(834\) m-qg (exact); the delta method in \(p_0\) collapses near there (\(p_0=0.917\)), so use Bayes–Haar (Ch. 8).

For thermal states the energy-basis measurement commutes with \(\rho\) and attains the QFI, equivalent to \(\operatorname{Var}(H)/T^4\); the delta-method warning is practical: with \(p_1\simeq0.083\), the Gaussian approximation needs \(Np_1\gg1\) and a Bayes–Haar interval is safer for small samples.

Sources for this section. “Quantum Fisher information matrix and multiparameter estimation” (2019), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08037; UMBC thesis on quantum thermometry, \(q=\tanh(\Delta/2T)\) and uniqueness of the optimal \(T\), https://api.mdsoar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2a193f11-5911-4759-928c-1354294bd751/content.

11.4 Clock comparison and gravitational shift

\[\Delta\phi=\frac{\omega\,T\,g\,\Delta h}{c^2},\qquad N(z\sigma)=\Big(\frac{z}{C\,\Delta\phi}\Big)^2\ \text{(reading at the equator)}.\] Sr clock (\(\omega=2\pi\cdot429.228\) THz), \(T=1\) s, \(\Delta h=1\) cm: \(\Delta\phi=2.943\cdot10^{-3}\) rad \(=0.468\) qga; \(N(3\sigma)=1.04\cdot10^6\) shots with \(C=1\), \(4.16\cdot10^6\) with \(C=0.5\) (exact; the real experiment §126 is referenced, not reproduced here).

With equator readout and \(N\) independent shots, \(\sigma_\phi=1/(C\sqrt N)\) and \(N=(z/(C\Delta\phi))^2\). The estimate is a planning one, with constant \(g\) and no dead time; Bothwell et al. measured the gradient within a millimetre-scale Sr sample with fractional uncertainty \(7.6\times10^{-21}\), but that experimental result is not reproduced here.

Sources for this section. T. Bothwell et al., “Resolving the gravitational redshift across a millimetre-scale atomic sample”, Nature 602, 420–424 (2022), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04349-7.

11.6 JAX block (qang/qmetro.py)

@jax.jit
def ramsey_fisher_phi(phi, C):          # I_F(φ) = C² sin²φ / (1 − C² cos²φ)
    return C ** 2 * jnp.sin(phi) ** 2 / (1.0 - C ** 2 * jnp.cos(phi) ** 2)

@jax.jit
def ghz_fisher_omega(n, T, T2):         # n² T² e^{−2nT/T2}
    return n ** 2 * T ** 2 * jnp.exp(-2.0 * n * T / T2)

@jax.jit
def product_fisher_omega(n, T, T2):     # n T² e^{−2T/T2}
    return n * T ** 2 * jnp.exp(-2.0 * T / T2)

def optimal_T(fisher_fn, T2, n):        # max_T I_F(ω)/T by descent in log T (jax.grad)
    f = lambda lT: -(fisher_fn(jnp.asarray(n), jnp.exp(lT), jnp.asarray(T2)) / jnp.exp(lT))
    g = jax.jit(jax.grad(f)); lT = jnp.log(jnp.asarray(T2) / 2.0)
    for _ in range(2000): lT = lT - 0.1 * g(lT)
    return float(jnp.exp(lT)), float(-f(lT))

@jax.jit
def thermal_fisher_x(x):                # ¼ sech²(x/2) via jax.grad of tanh(x/2)
    q = jnp.tanh(x / 2); dq = jax.grad(lambda t: jnp.tanh(t / 2))(x)
    return dq ** 2 / (1.0 - q ** 2)

def shots_to_resolve_height(omega, T, dh, z=3.0, C=1.0, g=9.80665, c=299792458.0):
    return (z / (C * omega * T * g * dh / c ** 2)) ** 2

11.9 Experiment 11.1 — Ramsey operating point at \(qg_Z=0\)

Hypothesis. The Ramsey Fisher information \(I_F(\phi)=C^2\sin^2\phi/(1-C^2\cos^2\phi)\) peaks at \(C^2\) when \(\phi=\pi/2\), i.e. \(qg_Z=0\) (250 qga). At \(\phi=0.2\) rad it loses 90 % of \(I_F\) for \(C=0.8\) and 95 % for \(C=0.5\); with \(C=1\), \(I_F\equiv1\).

Method. tools/verify_exp11_1.py scans 2001 \(\phi\) values with qang.qmetro.ramsey_fisher_phi, checks optimal_T, and sets the shot scale with qang.probability.shot_noise_milliqang.

C=1.0: max I_F=1.000000 at φ=3.1222 rad; at φ=0.2 loses -0.0 %
C=0.8: max I_F=0.640000 at φ=1.5708 rad; at φ=0.2 loses 89.8 %
C=0.5: max I_F=0.250000 at φ=1.5708 rad; at φ=0.2 loses 94.8 %
C=1: I_F≡1 (σ=3.4e-14); T*=T2/2=0.500000
N=20000: σ_m=7.0711 m-qg; σ_qga≈1.1254; 1 m-qg resolution → 1000000 shots

Reading. For \(C=1\) the maximum is flat in \(\phi\) and the numerical argmax is not informative; with decoherence the optimum is anchored at \(\pi/2\). The operational rule is to set the working point by \(qg_Z=0\) in m-qg, not by a nominal \(\phi\). exact; hardware unverified.
