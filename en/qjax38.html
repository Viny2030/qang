Chapter 38 — Cross-Cutting Applications Read with qang: Fermions and Jordan–Wigner, Hahn Echo, Transverse-Field Ising, Aharonov–Bohm, Wigner, Fresnel and the Unruh Vacuum

Updated: November 16, 2026

Partial rewrite of the cross-cutting chapter on the basis of Ch. 1–37. Module: qang.qtransv (exact NumPy). Verification: tools/verify_cap38.py. It covers seven of the thirteen phenomena of the previous chapter; the others (H₂ UCC, Kitaev chain, Jarzynski, GHZ Fisher, Fubini–Study, OTOC, level ladders) are cited without re-running (unverified). Everything is a closed formula, exact diagonalization or 2×2/4×4 algebra: no figure comes from an experiment. Abstract. Seven phenomena are recomputed exactly: the fermionic exchange sign is the difference between the two poles of \(qg_X\) (\(-1000\) vs \(+1000\) m-qg); the Hahn echo reads \(qg_X=1.000000\) for static detuning and 179 m-qg lower under 10 % drift; the transverse-field Ising peak sits at \(\theta=\pi/4\) only in the thermodynamic limit; Aharonov–Bohm gives \(qg_Z=\cos(2\pi\Phi/\Phi_0)\); Wigner rotation \(18.92^\circ\); Fresnel; and \(\theta/2=\operatorname{gd}(r)\) for the Unruh vacuum weight. Six others are cited, not re-run.

38.1 Reading rules

  1. qg has no period. \(qg_Z\in[-1,1]\) and the angle \(\theta\in[0,\pi]\). A criterion such as “phase \(=\pi\equiv0.5\) qg cycle” is rewritten as a reading (e.g. \(qg_X=-1\), \(-1000\) m-qg). qga (1 turn = 1000 qga) is periodic, but it is still an angle, not a reading.
  2. A phase does not change \(qg_Z\). A rotation about \(Z\) (Larmor, Aharonov–Bohm, exchange sign) leaves the populations intact: it is read in \(qg_X\) or \(qg_Y\) (Ch. 10, ex-7).
  3. Where there is a probability, there is a reading: reflectance, weight of a state, vacuum occupation.

38.2 Jordan–Wigner: the exchange sign is a reading

With \(a_j^\dagger=\big(\prod_{k<j}Z_k\big)\sigma_j^+\) (jw_creation), the relations \(\{a_i,a_j^\dagger\}=\delta_{ij}\) and \(\{a_i,a_j\}=0\) hold with error 0.0 for 4 modes (anticommutation_error). In a Hadamard test where an auxiliary qubit controls the exchange of occupied modes 0 and 1 (exchange_qgx): with the fermionic exchange (SWAP·CZ) the auxiliary reads \(qg_X=-1000\) m-qg; with a qubit SWAP, \(+1000\) m-qg. The Pauli sign is the difference between the poles of \(qg_X\). The \(Z\) gates of the parity string are exact: the error of a real circuit comes from noise, not from the mapping.

38.3 Larmor and Hahn echo

Free precession is a rotation about \(Z\): \(qg_Z=0\) always and \(qg_X=\cos\Delta\varphi\). With Gaussian detuning \(\sigma=2\pi\cdot50\) kHz and \(\tau=20\) µs (\(\sigma\tau=6.283\)), averaged by exact quadrature (hahn_echo_ensemble, no Monte Carlo):

case \(qg_X\) loss
free decay (\(\pi/2\), wait \(\tau\)) \(2.7\cdot10^{-9}\) —
echo, static detuning 1.000000 0
echo, \(\pi\) with 2 % extra 0.99901 0.99 m-qg
echo, detuning drifts 10 % of \(\sigma\) between halves 0.8209 (\(=e^{-(0.1\sigma\tau)^2/2}\)) 179 m-qg

The criterion “\(F=1\) independent of \(\Delta\omega\)” holds only with static detuning and exact pulses. Echo calibration with the device itself is in Ch. 30 (ex-27).

38.4 Transverse-field Ising: the peak shifts with size

\(H=-J\sum Z_iZ_{i+1}-g\sum X_i\), open chain; the transition is at \(g=J\) (\(\theta=\pi/4\)) in the thermodynamic limit. Exact diagonalization (tfim_mx_mqg) of the mean transverse reading at \(g=J\):

\(N\) 2 4 6 8 10 \(\infty\)
\(m_X\) (m-qg) 894.4 810.1 771.1 747.9 732.3 \(2000/\pi=636.6\)

It decreases monotonically toward the exact value, but with 10 spins it is still 95.6 m-qg above. The test of finding the transition with 2 to 4 spins fails: what gives \(\theta=\pi/4\) is the limit, not the scale (the positions of the entanglement-entropy peak, 0.33 and 0.65 for \(N=2\) and 4, come from the previous chapter and were not re-run).

38.5 Aharonov–Bohm

\(p_0=\cos^2(\pi\Phi/\Phi_0)\) ⇒ \(qg_Z=\cos(2\pi\Phi/\Phi_0)\) (ab_qgz), with \(\theta=2\pi\Phi/\Phi_0\) folded into \([0,\pi]\): what is periodic is the flux, not qg (period \(\Phi_0\) to \(2\cdot10^{-15}\)). With 2000 electrons per measurement and the exact binomial distribution, \(\sqrt N\,\sigma(\hat\Phi/\Phi_0)=0.1592\)–\(0.1598\) for \(\Phi/\Phi_0\in[0.05,0.45]\), against the bound \(1/2\pi=0.1592\): it is the arcsine transformation of Ch. 1 in an interferometer. The reading does not give the sign: \(\Phi\) and \(-\Phi\) read the same; \(qg_Y\) is needed.

38.6 Wigner rotation

Two non-collinear boosts, \(0.6c\,\hat x\) and \(0.8c\,\hat y\) (boost, wigner_rotation): polar decomposition of \(\Lambda=B_2B_1\) and closed formula agree to \(10^{-14}\): \(\Omega_W=0.3303\) rad \(=18.92^\circ\); a spin that pointed along \(x\) reads \(qg_X=\cos\Omega_W=+945.9\) m-qg; in qga, \(\Omega_W=52.6\) qga. With collinear boosts, \(\Omega_W=0\). It is Lorentz-group structure: the scale only translates the angle into a spin reading.

38.7 Fresnel

Air → glass (\(n=1.5\)) and glass → air (fresnel, tir_phase_difference):

  • Brewster, \(56.31^\circ\): \(r_p=0\) exactly; \(R_s=0.148\) (−704.1 m-qg). Normal incidence: \(R=0.04\) (−920 m-qg).
  • Total reflection (critical \(41.81^\circ\)): \(|r_s|=|r_p|=1\) to \(10^{-14}\); the reflectance reads \(+1000\) m-qg and conveys nothing: all the information is in the phases. \(|\delta_p-\delta_s|\) reaches \(45.24^\circ\) at \(51.67^\circ\) and equals \(45^\circ\) at \(50.23^\circ\) and \(53.26^\circ\) (Fresnel rhomb). With linear light at \(45^\circ\) incident at \(45^\circ\): \(qg_Y=\sin|\delta_p-\delta_s|=+600\) m-qg, a Stokes reading (Ch. 9, ex-6).
  • Goos–Hänchen is not a phase shift but a lateral displacement, from the derivative of the phase (not re-run here).

38.8 Unruh vacuum: the half-angle is the Gudermannian

Each reduced mode is thermal with \(\tanh^2r=e^{-2\pi\omega c/a}\). The vacuum weight is \(p_0=1/\cosh^2r\), and then \(\theta/2=\operatorname{gd}(r)=\arctan(\sinh r)\) to \(2\cdot10^{-16}\) (vacuum_weight, gudermannian). The closed entropy \(S=\cosh^2r\ln\cosh^2r-\sinh^2r\ln\sinh^2r\) agrees with the sum over occupation numbers to \(7\cdot10^{-16}\) up to \(r=1\) and to \(2\cdot10^{-6}\) at \(r=1.5\) (cutoff of 80 levels). An \(SU(1,1)\) transformation is not a rotation: the identity holds for the vacuum weight, not for the transformation.
