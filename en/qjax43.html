Chapter 43 — Quantum Robotics and Autonomous Control Read with qang: One Qubit per Joint, the Shot Budget, Real Time and Grover for Trajectory Selection

Día de análisis y actualización: 23 de noviembre de 2026

Analysis and update date: November 23, 2026

Abstract. What does qang give a robot, and what does it not? Each joint is encoded as a qubit, \(R_Y(\theta)|0\rangle\), and its qg values are read. Results, all with a verifier: (i) a \(Z\) reading gives the angle with variance \(1/N\) for every \(\theta\) but loses the sign; with two bases the sign is recovered and the variance is \((\cos^4\theta+\sin^4\theta)/N\), above the Cramér–Rao bound \(1/(2N)\) except at \(45^\circ\); (ii) the end-effector tolerance fixes the shots, \(N=\lceil z^2\,\mathrm{tr}(JJ^{\mathsf T})/\varepsilon^2\rceil\); (iii) the control cycle fixes the available shots: a two-link arm with tolerance \(0.02\) needs 7225 shots per joint, while at 100 Hz and 1 ms per shot only 10 fit; (iv) a drifting joint is tracked by a scalar Kalman filter with a closed-form steady-state variance; (v) Grover saves queries by \(\sim(2/\pi)\sqrt N\) in the oracle model. Status: exact for the identities and budgets; no measured gain is claimed. Module: qang.qrobot (NumPy). Verification: tools/verify_cap43.py.

43.1 Reading rules

  1. Every result comes with its function in qang.qrobot and its line in tools/verify_cap43.py. Scales: \(m\text{-qg}=1000\,qg_Z\) (reading); the joint angle is given in radians and, when convenient, in qga (1 turn \(=2\pi\) rad \(=1000\) qga; \(\pi/2\) rad \(=250\) qga).
  2. We optimize in \(\theta\) (or in qga), never in \(qg_Z\) without regularization: the flat gradient in \(qg_Z\) diverges at the poles (Ch. 34, THEORY_CORE.md §2.1).
  3. A shot time, a loop rate or an oracle cost is a parameter of the model, not a measurement of this book. Where a number appears, it is what the formula gives for that parameter.
  4. No measured gain status: the chapter compares neither against a real robot nor against a classical controller on data.

43.2 Theoretical foundations and mathematical formulation

State space of a joint. A planar arm with \(n\) links, lengths \(\ell_i\) and relative angles \(\theta_i\), has end effector

\[ \mathbf p(\boldsymbol\theta)=\Big(\sum_i \ell_i\cos\phi_i,\ \sum_i \ell_i\sin\phi_i\Big),\qquad \phi_i=\sum_{j\le i}\theta_j, \]

and Jacobian \(J_{\cdot i}=\partial\mathbf p/\partial\theta_i\). Each joint is encoded in one qubit, \(|\theta\rangle=R_Y(\theta)|0\rangle\), with

\[ p_0=\cos^2\frac\theta2,\qquad qg_Z=\cos\theta,\qquad qg_X=\sin\theta,\qquad m_Z=1000\cos\theta\ \text{m-qg}. \]

The full circuit is \(U(\boldsymbol\theta)=\bigotimes_i R_Y(\theta_i)\), a product: the decision state is separable and its parameter space is the joint torus \([-\pi,\pi]^n\).

Reading one joint. With \(N\) shots in \(Z\), \(\widehat{qg}_Z=2k_0/N-1\) with \(\mathrm{Var}(\widehat{qg}_Z)=\sin^2\theta/N\). Propagating through \(\hat\theta=\arccos\widehat{qg}_Z\),

\[ \mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)\simeq\frac{\sin^2\theta/N}{\sin^2\theta}=\frac1N\quad\text{for all }\theta\in(0,\pi), \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{qga}}=\frac{1000}{2\pi}\frac1{\sqrt N}=\frac{159.15}{\sqrt N}\ \text{qga}. \]

This is a constant Fisher information \(I_Z=N\): the angular error does not depend on where the joint is (unlike the error in \(m\)-qg, which vanishes at the poles; Ch. 1).

The sign trap. \(qg_Z=\cos\theta\) is even: \(\theta\) and \(-\theta\) give the same reading, and \(\arccos\) returns \([0,\pi]\) (Ch. 34, arccos_range_trap). A joint with a symmetric range needs a second basis. With \(N\) shots in \(Z\) and \(N\) in \(X\) (\(qg_X=\sin\theta\)),

\[ \hat\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\widehat{qg}_X,\widehat{qg}_Z),\qquad \mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)\simeq\frac{\cos^4\theta+\sin^4\theta}{N}\ \ge\ \frac1{2N}=\mathrm{CRB}(\theta), \]

with equality only at \(\theta=45^\circ\) (125 qga). The total Fisher information is \(N+N\), and the \(\operatorname{atan2}\) estimator does not saturate it except at \(45^\circ\).

From the axis to the end effector. With independent errors of \(\sigma_\theta=1/\sqrt N\) per joint, the mean-square error of the end effector to first order is

\[ \mathrm{RMS}(\mathbf p)=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{tr}(JJ^{\mathsf T})}{N}},\qquad N(\varepsilon)=\Big\lceil \frac{z^2\,\mathrm{tr}(JJ^{\mathsf T})}{\varepsilon^2}\Big\rceil . \]

This is the shot budget before executing the action: it depends on the posture (through \(J\)), not on the unit.

Real-time budget. With loop rate \(f\), time per shot \(t_s\) and overhead \(t_0\), the shots per cycle are \(N_c=\lfloor(1/f-t_0)/t_s\rfloor\) and \(\sigma_\theta=1/\sqrt{N_c}\). The loop meets the tolerance only if \(N_c\ge N(\varepsilon)\), that is, if \(f\le 1/(t_0+N(\varepsilon)\,t_s)\).

A drifting joint. If \(\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+w\) (\(\mathrm{Var}\,w=q\)) and each cycle delivers \(\hat\theta_t\) with variance \(r=1/N\), the scalar Kalman filter (Kalman 1960) has steady-state prior variance \(P^-=\tfrac12\big(q+\sqrt{q^2+4qr}\big)\) and posterior variance \(P=P^--q\). What qang contributes is the value of \(r\) (\(1/N\), or \((159.15)^2/N\) qga²); the filter is classical.

Trajectory selection with Grover. With \(N_c\) candidates of which \(M\) are collision-free (\(\sin^2\theta_0=M/N_c\)), \(k\) iterations give success \(\sin^2\big((2k+1)\theta_0\big)\) (Ch. 3), with \(k^\star=\lfloor\pi/(4\theta_0)\rfloor\). Unstructured classical search needs \((N_c+1)/(M+1)\) expected evaluations, and \(\Omega(\sqrt{N_c/M})\) queries is a quantum lower bound in the oracle model (Bennett et al. 1997; Grover 1996). The classical/quantum ratio grows as \((2/\pi)\sqrt{N_c}\) for \(M=1\).

Variational optimization. A parametric circuit (Kandala et al. 2017; Cerezo et al. 2021) in inverse kinematics minimizes \(C(\boldsymbol\theta)=\lVert\mathbf p(\boldsymbol\theta)-\mathbf p^\star\rVert^2\); the gradient of each reading comes from the parameter-shift rule, \(\partial qg_Z/\partial\theta=[qg_Z(\theta+\pi/2)-qg_Z(\theta-\pi/2)]/2=-\sin\theta\), with a shift of 250 qga. Since \(C\) is an exact classical function of \(\boldsymbol\theta\), the circuit only adds shot noise: that use stays unverified (§43.7).

43.3 Hybrid architecture and integration with qang v0.16

 target p*  ──►  [classical IK: two_link_ik / Jacobian]  ──►  θ_ref
                                  │
                                  ▼
                  [preflight: shots_for_end_effector, shots_per_cycle]   ← qang.qrobot
                       │ required N vs available N per cycle
              ┌────────┴─────────┐
        feasible = 1        feasible = 0  ──► lower loop rate f, raise ε, or run on a simulator
              │
              ▼
   [QPU/simulator]  R_Y(θ_i) per joint, measured in Z and in X   (Qiskit)
              │ counts
              ▼
   [qang: qg_Z, qg_X → atan2 → θ̂_i, σ in qga]  ──►  [scalar Kalman]  ──►  actuator

The flow is classical–quantum–classical: the quantum part happens only in the middle box, and what qang adds is the pre-check (how many shots, how much error, whether it fits the cycle) and the reading with its uncertainty in qga. Kinematics and the filter are classical and are not replaced.

43.4 Practical implementation (qang + Qiskit)

import numpy as np
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.primitives import StatevectorSampler          # exact Qiskit 2.x simulator
from qang.qrobot import (two_link_ik, planar_fk, preflight, joint_from_readings, joint_sigma_qga)

LINKS, TARGET = (1.0, 0.8), (1.2, 0.9)
theta_ref = two_link_ik(TARGET, *LINKS)                   # (0.1279, 1.1796) rad: classical reference

# 1. Pre-check, before touching the environment (illustrative parameters: 1 ms per shot)
fast = preflight(theta_ref, LINKS, eps=0.02, rate_hz=100, t_shot_s=1e-3)
slow = preflight(theta_ref, LINKS, eps=0.02, rate_hz=0.1, t_shot_s=1e-3)
print(fast["shots_required"], fast["shots_available"], fast["feasible"])   # 7225.0 10.0 0.0
print(slow["feasible"], round(slow["sigma_theta_qga"], 2))                  # 1.0 1.59

# 2. Reading per joint: R_Y(θ)|0>, two bases to recover the sign
sampler = StatevectorSampler(seed=43)

def read_joint(theta, n):
    qg = []
    for basis in ("Z", "X"):
        qc = QuantumCircuit(1, 1)
        qc.ry(theta, 0)
        if basis == "X":
            qc.h(0)                                       # measure X: H before measuring
        qc.measure(0, 0)
        counts = sampler.run([qc], shots=n).result()[0].data.c.get_counts()
        qg.append(2 * counts.get("0", 0) / n - 1)
    return joint_from_readings(*qg)                       # atan2(qg_X, qg_Z)

n = int(slow["shots_required"])
theta_hat = [read_joint(t, n) for t in theta_ref]
err = np.linalg.norm(planar_fk(theta_hat, LINKS) - planar_fk(theta_ref, LINKS))
print(round(err, 3), "versus tolerance 0.02")              # a single run: of the order of 0.02 or less

Interpretation.

  • The pre-check is deterministic and reproducible: for the target \((1.2,\,0.9)\) and \(\varepsilon=0.02\), 7225 shots per joint are needed. At 100 Hz and 1 ms per shot, 10 fit, \(\sigma_\theta=0.316\) rad (\(18.1^\circ\), \(50.3\) qga) and the end-effector error would be \(0.54\). At \(0.1\) Hz, 10 000 shots fit, \(\sigma_\theta=0.01\) rad (\(1.59\) qga) and the loop is feasible.
  • The two-basis reading costs \(2N\) shots per joint; its expected error is \(\le\varepsilon\) (at \(45^\circ\) it is half the variance of the \(Z\)-only case). A single run fluctuates around that RMS: it is not a per-run guarantee.
  • The print values of step 2 are not a measurement of this book. Qiskit was not installed in the environment where the chapter was written; the identities behind it (variances, RMS and budget) are verified with NumPy binomial shots in tools/verify_cap43.py. Whoever reproduces the block should check the Qiskit output in their own environment.

Tracking a drifting joint (verified in tools/verify_cap43.py, §[5]): with \(q=4\cdot10^{-4}\) rad² per cycle and \(N=400\) shots, the steady-state posterior variance is \(8.20\cdot10^{-4}\) rad² (simulated: \(8.14\cdot10^{-4}\)), that is, \(\sigma=0.029\) rad versus \(0.050\) rad without the filter.

43.5 Use cases, limitations (NISQ versus simulators) and future directions

Use cases with support.

decision how qang.qrobot helps support
how many shots to request per joint for an end-effector tolerance \(N(\varepsilon)\) depending on posture shots_for_end_effector
whether a loop fits the cycle before launching it preflight shots_per_cycle, max_control_rate
whether a joint with a symmetric range requires two bases sign trap joint_from_readings
how much tracking error a drift leaves closed-form steady-state variance steady_state_variance
how many queries Grover saves and up to what oracle cost it breaks even \((2/\pi)\sqrt N\) and break-even point grover_query_ratio

Limitations. (i) Real time: with millisecond shot times, a tolerance of \(0.01\) rad on one joint limits the loop to \(0.1\) Hz; with the queue time of a cloud service, worse. A kHz control loop cannot be closed today with quantum readings by shots. (ii) NISQ: the model is one noiseless qubit per joint; with readout noise the affine readout must be calibrated (qang.qchar, Ch. 30); the gate noise of an \(R_Y\) is small, but the readout noise is not. (iii) Grover: the saving is in queries, and it requires a coherent collision oracle whose cost does not enter \((2/\pi)\sqrt N\); Chapter 3 measures that with noise, reading \(qg_Z\) per qubit loses against the string histogram (66 % versus 91 % at \(n=5\)). (iv) Variational: the kinematics is an exact classical function; a parametric circuit does not improve it (§43.7).

Future directions, without result. The audited inventory of domains (dataset, without-qang baseline and status) is in book/en/appendix_h.qmd (Open and rejected proposals) and tools/check_baselines.py. Quantum SLAM: qang has no verified formulation; it would be a hypothesis for a pre-registered experiment (data association as a search in a weight sector would be a hypothesis), unverified. Optimal control under uncertainty: the possible contribution is the \(r=1/N\) of the measurement inside a classical LQG; no advantage is claimed. Arms with more degrees of freedom: \(N(\varepsilon)\) grows with \(\mathrm{tr}(JJ^{\mathsf T})\), that is, with the reach of the arm.

43.6 Without qang and with qang

action without qang with qang difference
fix the shots per joint trial and error on the end effector \(N=\lceil z^2\,\mathrm{tr}(JJ^{\mathsf T})/\varepsilon^2\rceil\) before running prior budget; no gain in the error
read the angle of a joint \(\arccos(\widehat{qg}_Z)\): loses the sign \(\operatorname{atan2}(\widehat{qg}_X,\widehat{qg}_Z)\) with two bases correction; costs \(2N\) shots
know whether the loop is possible “real time” without quantification \(N_c\) versus \(N(\varepsilon)\) exact check, no gain
track a drift Kalman filter with an assumed \(r\) the same filter with \(r=1/N\) (or in qga²) identification of \(r\); the filter is classical
choose a free trajectory classical sampling, \((N_c+1)/(M+1)\) Grover, \(\sim\sqrt{N_c/M}\) queries query saving in the oracle model; no measured gain

Balance. qang turns the question “can a qubit read the angle of a joint?” into numbers: how many shots, how much error, which cycle. The honest answer is that, today, the real-time budget rules out the fast loop, and that Grover’s advantage is in queries. There is no measured gain.

43.7 Verification — tools/verify_cap43.py

python tools/verify_cap43.py          # NumPy + qang only; ~2 min
pytest tests/test_qrobot.py -q        # 8 tests, ~1 s

Seven blocks: [1] kinematics (Jacobian by finite differences, 400 IK solutions, out-of-reach target); [2] variance \(1/N\) and \((\cos^4\theta+\sin^4\theta)/N\) with binomial shots (\(\pm10\%\), 5 angles) and the Cramér–Rao bound; [3] end-effector RMS (\(N=500\) and \(4000\), \(\pm6\%\)) and \(N(\varepsilon)=43\,342\) shots per joint for \(\varepsilon=0.01\) with three links; [4] real-time budget and preflight (7225 required, 10 available at 100 Hz); [5] scalar Kalman (\(\pm20\%\)); [6] Grover: formula versus explicit simulation in four cases, simulated classical \((N+1)/(M+1)\) and ratio \(651\approx(2/\pi)\sqrt{2^{20}}\); [7] parameter shift \(-\sin\theta\) with 250 qga.

The reproducible validation of this review is verify_cap43: OK: finite-difference Jacobian and closed-form IK (400 solutions), \(\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)=1/N\) and \((\cos^4\theta+\sin^4\theta)/N\ge1/(2N)\) simulated to \(\pm10\%\) with equality only at \(45^\circ\), end-effector RMS to \(\pm6\%\) with \(N(0.01)=43\,342\), preflight of \(7225\) required and \(10\) available shots at 100 Hz, Kalman with \(P=8.20\cdot10^{-4}\) rad² and Grover with \(P=0.99946\) and ratio \(651\); tests/test_qrobot.py passes with 8 passed.
