\begin{abstract}
\noindent Earlier records of this project introduced the Qang ($qg$) coordinates and a reference implementation. This record refounds the work on the complete version~0.16.0 of the open-source library \texttt{qang} and its book (42 chapters, Appendices A--H). The claim is deliberately narrower than in earlier presentations. Qang adds no new physical observable: $\qgZ=\langle\sigma_z\rangle$ and $\qgS=h_2((1+\qgZ)/2)$ are exact functions of one outcome probability. What the library contributes is (i) two explicit scales, the angular \st{qga} (1~turn $=1000$~qga) and the reading \st{m-qg} ($1000\,\qgZ$); (ii) a catalogue of design rules, each tied to one function in \texttt{qang} and one verification script, that reduce measurement settings, ancillas, entangling gates, pulses or shots in the cases where the Z-basis reading is exact; and (iii) a ledger of 179 sub-measures with a status each. In the ledger, 145 are \st{exact}, 5 are \st{tested on hardware} (one device), none is a \st{measured gain}, and 29 are recorded as \st{against}, \st{no gain}, \st{mixed}, \st{not adopted} or \st{unverified}. We summarize the utility field by field, state the structural limits (including a no-go theorem for any statistic of a single product-basis distribution), and give the falsification protocol that would retire each claim.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Resumen.} Esta refundación reúne la versión 0.16.0 completa de \texttt{qang} y su libro (42 capítulos). Qang no introduce un observable físico nuevo; aporta dos escalas explícitas (\st{qga}, \st{m-qg}), reglas de diseño que reducen ajustes de medida, ancillas, compuertas, pulsos o disparos donde la lectura en base~Z es exacta, y un libro de cuentas de 179 submedidas con estatus. Ninguna figura como \st{measured gain}; sólo cinco están \st{tested on hardware}, en un único dispositivo. Se resumen los usos por campo, los límites estructurales y el protocolo de falsación.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is being refounded}
Version 0.15.0 (PyPI, 41 chapters) was the previous release; version 0.16.0 adds Chapter 42 and the single-basis results of Chapter 34. Earlier Zenodo records of this project (10.5281/zenodo.22832150, 10.5281/zenodo.23036963, 10.5281/zenodo.23989993~\cite{z1,z2,z3}) are superseded by this one for the state of the library and the book. Since then three things changed. First, the book was rewritten as verified chapters 7--42: every number in a chapter comes from a function in \texttt{qang} and a script \texttt{tools/verify\_capXX.py}, and the same number is pinned by a test. Second, every sub-measure received a status from a closed vocabulary (\st{exact}, \st{measured gain}, \st{tested on hardware}, \st{mixed}, \st{no gain}, \st{against}, \st{unverified}, \st{not adopted}) in Appendix~H, and negative findings are kept, not removed. Third, the angular scale was separated from the reading scale, so that no result depends on a unit convention.

The refoundation therefore changes the \emph{kind} of claim: from ``a parametrization with advantages'' to ``a measurement-level vocabulary whose each rule says where it is exact, what it saves, and where it must not be used''.

\section{Definitions and scales}
For a qubit with outcome probabilities $p_0,p_1$: $\qgZ=p_0-p_1=2p_0-1=\cos\theta$ for a pure state with polar angle $\theta$, and $\qgS=h_2(p_0)$ in bits. The exact identity $\qgS=h_2((1+\qgZ)/2)$ holds for every single-qubit state; $\qgS$ fixes $|\qgZ|$ but not its sign, so it carries no independent single-qubit information. The two scales are distinct:
\begin{itemize}
\item \st{qga}: angular scale, 1 turn $=2\pi$ rad $=1000$ qga (pole $|0\rangle=0$, equator $=250$, pole $|1\rangle=500$).
\item \st{m-qg}: reading $m=1000\,\qgZ=2000\,p_0-1000\in[-1000,1000]$, linear in $p_0$ and non-linear in angle.
\end{itemize}
For a mixed qubit with Bloch vector $\mathbf r$, $\arccos(\qgZ)$ is an effective population angle, not the geometric polar angle. A readout channel acts as the affine map $m'=(1-e_0-e_1)m+1000(e_1-e_0)$, which is why the same reading can be corrected from two calibration poles (Ch.~35).

\section{Structural limits and recorded negatives}
\paragraph{Single-basis no-go.} For any distribution $p$, $\sigma_p=\sum_xp(x)|x\rangle\langle x|$ is fully separable and has exactly that distribution. Hence no rule using only $p$ ($\qgZ$, $\qgS$, joint outcome entropy $H_Z$, Z-basis total correlation $C_Z$, or any learned summary) can certify entanglement, even at infinite shots (Ch.~34, \st{diagonal\_twin}). Verified examples with the same uniform $P_Z$: the product $|{+}{+}\rangle$, the edge graph state and $I/4$; for three qubits, $|{+}{+}{+}\rangle$, the path graph state and $I/8$. $C_Z=0$ occurs with the entangled graph states, and $C_Z=2$ bits (the maximum) with a separable 000/111 mixture. A two-setting witness does certify the three-qubit path: $W'_3=2I-XZI-ZXZ-IZX$ equals $-1$ on the graph state and $+2$ on the product and on $I/8$; with 1000 shots per setting and $\delta=0.05$ the conservative radius is $0.2936$, so the violation is certified (\st{witness\_path}, \st{witness\_radius}). The compatible purity range from a Z distribution is $[\sum_xp^2,1]$. Sufficient (not necessary) shot bounds to estimate $H_Z$ or $C_Z$ within $0.05$ bit at $\delta=0.05$ are $1.3\cdot10^5$--$4.0\cdot10^6$; the plug-in $C_Z$ has bias $(2^n-1-n)/(2N\ln2)$, which is a statistical artefact, not correlation. These identities and numbers are checked in \texttt{tools/verify\_cap34.py}.

\paragraph{Other recorded negatives.} Direct descent in $\qgZ$ does not converge near the poles (0/20) without regularization; the unit change qga does not mitigate barren plateaus or kernel concentration; the Page curve with $\qgS$ is flat; local readings are flat for a free-boson CFT, so $\qgZ$ and $\qgS$ do not measure the central charge (which is recovered only from the full correlation matrix); classical shadows need $3$--$41\times$ more shots than direct Z measurement for the quantities used here; post-quantum cryptography has no qubits to read; and the weight filter does not detect weight-conserving errors (retention $1.0$, shift $-174.7$ m-qg unseen). The relativistic-energy proposal (Appendix~G) and physical closed timelike curves remain \st{unverified}.

\section{Hardware evidence}\label{sec:hw}
The only runs on a physical device are the stored IQM Garnet experiments of Chapter~35; they were not repeated on a second device. (i) Three-flavour neutrino circuits, $8\times300$ shots: filtered mean error $0.0615\to0.0386$ ($\times1.59$) with the survival minimum recovered at 2.6~GeV. (ii) One-qubit milli-qang scan, $26\times4000$ shots: one X gate costs $1.64$ m-qg ($\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=0.81$); equator reading $+24.95\to+1.5$ m-qg after correction with the two poles. (iii) A Dirac-qubit state at $0.6c$ read $+759.9\pm14.9$ m-qg against $+800$ predicted; the relativistic interpretation is not adopted as verified. No other chapter claims hardware evidence, and Appendix~H marks every other device statement as simulation, cited or \st{unverified}.

\section{Falsification and reproducibility}
\paragraph{Pre-registered protocol (Ch.~31).} Thresholds and predictions are fixed before measurement and hashed: all 9 of 9 protocol fields change the hash when altered. Over 20 simultaneous hypotheses the false-positive rate falls from $0.656$ to $0.042$ with Holm control; a smaller effect ($\Delta=0.02$ instead of $0.1$) costs $24.3\times$ more shots. The prior predictions of the research notes (26 of 45 fulfilled, \S157--\S164) are cited, not re-run, and are marked \st{unverified}.
\paragraph{What would retire a claim.} A claim in a row of Section~4 is retired if (a) its verifier fails or its number moves beyond the stated tolerance; (b) a pre-registered device run on a second backend shows no saving within its stated interval; or (c) a counterexample is added to Ch.~34. This record adopts the following rule: status moves only by that evidence: \st{exact} $\to$ \st{tested on hardware} requires a device run; \st{tested on hardware} $\to$ \st{measured gain} requires replication on a different device against the pre-registered thresholds. No row has been promoted beyond \st{tested on hardware}.
\paragraph{Reproducibility.} \texttt{pip install qang} (core: NumPy only); \texttt{python tools/verify\_capNN.py} for each chapter; \texttt{pytest} for the suite (SDK tests skip when a package is absent); \texttt{python tools/check\_book\_api.py .} compares every code block of the book with the public API signatures. Extras with incompatible pins (\texttt{iqm}, \texttt{dynamics}, JAX with \texttt{jax<0.9}) live in their own environments.

\section{Open items}
(1) A replication on a second device and a pre-registered run of the filters of Ch.~12, 23 and 24. (2) Hardware tests of the sector filters beyond the three-flavour case. (3) Closed-form uncertainty for estimating $H_Z$ and $C_Z$ with calibrated multinomial intervals, to replace the loose sufficient bounds. (4) Physical reading of the Dirac-qubit and time-loop chapters. (5) The English edition of the book still follows the earlier numbering and lacks the verified chapters 7--42.

\section{Conclusion}
Version 0.16.0 is a library of measurement-level design rules with an audit trail. Its utility is concrete and bounded: fewer settings, ancillas, gates or shots in the cases where the Z reading is exact, sector filters for weight-conserving circuits, and planning bounds; its limits are as explicit as its uses. The refoundation consists in treating both with equal weight.
